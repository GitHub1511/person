## 16. Locomotion, manipulation, speech, and a mind

This part was added on top of the body described above.  It is deliberately
presented in terms of what was *measured*, because several components are
demonstrations and not solved problems.

### 16.1 Whole-body control (`wbc.py`)

Standing is now controlled by inverse dynamics instead of the equilibrium-point
spring.  Every 4 ms, the whole-body controller (WBC) solves one least-squares
problem for the joint accelerations and contact wrenches.  The problem is to
track a desired centre-of-mass (COM) acceleration, the pelvis and chest
orientation, the swing-foot trajectory and a posture reference, subject to the
floating-base dynamics, unilateral contacts, a centre-of-pressure box inside
each foot, a friction cone and a torsional-friction limit.  Violated inequality
constraints are handled with an active set (pins).  Joint torques are clipped to
the human limits specified in `skeleton.py`.  The eyes, jaw and fingers remain
under their own controllers.

### 16.2 Bipedal walking (`locomotion.py`)

A divergent-component-of-motion (capture-point) planner places each foot, with
`beta_step` scaling the aggressiveness of the placement; the states are
`stand → init → unload → single support ↔ double support → settle → stand`.
Step length is integrated so as to meet the commanded speed, swing feet follow
minimum-jerk trajectories, and the heading is drawn towards the commanded
heading.  Approximately 25 gait and WBC parameters were tuned by random search
(parallel, eight rounds).

**Measured result: partly functional.**

* For the best parameter set, evaluated over 13 s of straight walking at
  0.3 m/s with four noise seeds, the walker **survived 2 of 4** runs; the
  remaining runs fell between 8 and 9 s (≈1.5–2 m).  Across the whole sweep,
  only 18 of 224 candidates survived even one seed, and the 18 survivors, when
  re-tested under new seeds, mostly scored 0/4; the sweep therefore
  over-fits its seeds.
* When walking for 5 s and then stopping was evaluated with eight seeds,
  **2 of 8** runs remained upright; for three bursts of 3.5 s with intervening
  stops, **1 of 8** did.  Stopping and turning are the weakest components.
* A bounded virtual pelvis stabiliser was tried; it did not help, and it is
  therefore not included in the code.
* Standing (including standing while the arms are moved) is dependable; walking
  should be regarded as limited to "a few steps", and the skill layer will fall
  over if it is asked to walk further.  `walk_to` exists but inherits this
  unreliability.
* The tuning was performed with the table moved out of the way; a long walk past
  furniture was not tested.

### 16.3 Arms, hands, grasping, and holding (`skills.py`)

* **Arm inverse kinematics (IK)** is analytic (the law of cosines for the
  shoulder–elbow–wrist chain, a search over the swivel angle within the joint
  limits, and the wrist determined from the residual orientation), supplemented
  by a small integral term for gravity sag and by a trunk lean that is permitted
  only while the COM remains over the feet.
* **Hands**: the fingers are rigid and have two phalanges each, arranged in three
  digit groups (thumb, index, other fingers) with named poses (`open, relaxed,
  fist, point, pinch, thumbs_up, ok, grip`).  There is no opposable-thumb
  squeeze.
* **Grasp pipeline**: the sequence is to lift the hand clear of the table edge,
  approach beside the object, descend, slide the palm in, close the digits, and
  lift.
* **The grip is assisted, and this is a shortcut:** when the palm contacts the
  object, a soft weld constraint between hand and object is switched on.
  Without it, the rigid fingers squeeze the object out (an apple is ejected at
  ~0.4 m/s).  Release switches the weld off.  Pick-up therefore demonstrates the
  *behaviour* of grasping, not a physically earned force-closure grasp.
* **Gestures**: `wave, nod, shake_head, shrug, clap, thumbs_up, think,
  scratch_head, drink, bow`.  **Head and eyes**: neck yaw and pitch together with
  the eyes follow `look_at`, and wander when idle.
* Measured: the sequence grab → hold → put down succeeded for the apple and the
  mug in the scripted test at the table, with the person standing at the table.
  The stone (heavy, wide) could not be gripped, and one mug put-down run ended
  with the mug flung to the floor; the procedure should therefore be regarded as
  fragile.

### 16.4 Speech and thoughts (`speech.py`, `viewer.py`)

Speech is produced by opening and closing the jaw according to a syllable-timed
envelope (no fine mouth shape is produced).  The spoken content appears in a
white bubble above the head, and the thoughts of the person appear above the
head in grey italics.  The speech can optionally also be rendered aloud through
the Windows Speech Application Programming Interface (SAPI) (`--voice`).  There
is **no built-in drive to speak**; the person speaks only if the mind decides to
do so.

The jaw is moved kinematically, and its actuator is muted while speech is
produced (in an earlier version, the actuator opposed the override, and the
resulting reaction torque knocked the whole body over).

### 16.5 The mind (`mind.py`, `world.py`, `run_mind.py`, `setup_azr.py`)

The body exposes an application programming interface (API) of actions (`say, look_at, look_forward, hand_pose,
wait, walk_to, walk, turn, face, stop, reach, grab, release, put_down,
point_at, gesture, crouch, stand`).  `world.py` converts the simulator state into
what a person could know: objects in the field of view, described
egocentrically ("0.4 m ahead, 0.2 m to the right"), together with what the hands
hold and what was heard.  `mind.py` constructs a prompt from this description,
queries a language model, and parses its answer.

* **Absolute Zero Reasoner (AZR) format.**  AZR is a Qwen2.5-Coder model trained
  with an R1-style `<think>…</think><answer>…</answer>` format.  The prompt
  ends with `Assistant: <think>`, so that the model continues in this format;
  the text in `<think>` becomes the grey italic thought bubble, and the
  `<answer>` is parsed as lines of calls such as `look_at("apple")`.  The
  answer is **never executed**: it is parsed with `ast` against a whitelist of
  the API names, with literal arguments only.
* **Backends**: any OpenAI-compatible `/v1/completions` server (llama.cpp's
  `llama-server`, vLLM, Ollama) with streaming, `llama-cpp-python`, or
  `transformers`.  `setup_azr.py` describes how the weights are obtained; it
  downloads nothing unless `--download` is passed.
* **Incentive.**  The prompt states that the mind does not have to say or do
  anything.  There is no reward for speaking.

**What has and has not been run.  AZR itself has never been run here.**  No
weights are installed, so the live path is verified only against a small mock
OpenAI-compatible server that returns a pre-scripted `<think>/<answer>` stream
(`diag_mind_server.py`: prompt construction, Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP)
streaming, parsing, dispatch into the body, the thought bubble, and a finished
`grab('apple')` all function).  Without any model, `run_mind.py` falls back to a
**scripted stand-in** (`StubBackend`) that reacts to a few keywords and wanders;
it is *not* a language model, and the heads-up display (HUD) states this.  How a
real AZR model behaves as the mind of this person, and whether a 3B
code-reasoning model is suitable for that role, is unknown.  AZR was trained for
code and mathematical reasoning, not for embodied dialogue.

```bash
python run_mind.py                                   # window + scripted stand-in
python run_mind.py --at-table --backend server --url http://127.0.0.1:8080/v1
python run_mind.py --headless 45 --at-table --say "4:hello" --say "14:please pick up the apple"
python setup_azr.py                                  # what to do to get AZR
```

### 16.6 Limitations of this part

* Walking is unreliable (§16.2); stopping and turning are more unreliable still.
* Grasping relies on an assist weld; the fingers are rigid, there is no
  opposable thumb, and heavy or wide objects fail.
* The simulation runs at roughly 0.1–0.3× real time on the test machine
  (whole-body control every 4 ms plus rendering), so the window runs slowly.
* Several fixes were tuned against one scene layout (table at 0.2 m from the
  edge, objects at the positions assigned by `build_model.py`).
* The mind receives a symbolic description, not pixels; the 48×36 retina is
  unused by it.
* The diagnostic scripts for this part are `diag_wbc.py`, `diag_walk*.py`,
  `diag_skills.py`, `diag_bubbles.py`, `diag_mind_smoke.py`,
  `diag_mind_server.py`, `diag_app.py`.
