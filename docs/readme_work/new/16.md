## 16. Locomotion, manipulation, speech, and a mind

This part was added on top of the body described above.  It is deliberately
presented in terms of what was *measured*, because several components are
demonstrations and not solved problems.

### 16.1 Whole-body control (`wbc.py`)

Standing is now controlled by inverse dynamics, implemented as whole-body
control (WBC), instead of the equilibrium-point spring.  At intervals of 4 ms
the controller solves a single least-squares problem for the joint accelerations
and contact wrenches.  The objective is to track a desired centre-of-mass (COM)
acceleration, the pelvis and chest orientation, the swing-foot trajectory, and a
posture reference, subject to the floating-base dynamics, unilateral contacts, a
centre-of-pressure box inside each foot, a friction cone, and a torsional-friction
limit.  Violated inequality constraints are handled by means of an active set
(pins).  Joint torques are clipped to the human limits specified in `skeleton.py`.
The eyes, jaw, and fingers remain under their own controllers.

### 16.2 Bipedal locomotion (`locomotion.py`)

Foot placement is determined by a divergent-component-of-motion (capture-point)
planner, in which `beta_step` scales the aggressiveness of the placement.  The
gait cycles through the states
`stand → init → unload → single support ↔ double support → settle → stand`.
Step length is integrated so as to meet the commanded speed, swing feet follow
minimum-jerk trajectories, and the heading is drawn towards the commanded
heading.  Approximately 25 gait and WBC parameters were tuned by random search
(parallel, eight rounds).

**Measured result: partly functional.**
* For the best parameter set, evaluated over 13 s of straight walking at
  0.3 m/s with four noise seeds, the walker **survived 2 of 4** runs; the
  remaining runs fell between 8 and 9 s (≈1.5–2 m).  Across the whole sweep,
  only 18 of 224 candidates survived even a single seed, and the 18 survivors,
  when re-tested under new seeds, mostly scored 0/4.  The sweep therefore
  over-fits its seeds.
* When walking for 5 s and then stopping was evaluated with eight seeds,
  **2 of 8** runs remained upright; for three bursts of 3.5 s with intervening
  stops, **1 of 8** did.  Stopping and turning are the weakest components.
* A bounded virtual pelvis stabiliser was evaluated; it did not improve
  performance and is therefore not included in the code.
* Standing (including standing while the arms are moved) is reliable; walking
  should be regarded as limited to "a few steps", and the skill layer will fall
  over if it is asked to walk further.  `walk_to` exists but inherits this
  unreliability.
* The tuning was performed with the table moved out of the way; a long walk past
  furniture was not tested.

### 16.3 Arms, hands, grasping, and holding (`skills.py`)

* **Arm inverse kinematics (IK)** is solved analytically (the law of cosines for
  the shoulder–elbow–wrist chain, a search over the swivel angle within the joint
  limits, and the wrist determined from the residual orientation), supplemented
  by a small integral term for gravity sag and by a trunk lean that is permitted
  only while the COM remains over the feet.
* **Hands**: the fingers are rigid and comprise two phalanges each, organised
  into three digit groups (thumb, index, other fingers) with named poses (`open, relaxed, fist, point, pinch, thumbs_up, ok, grip`).  No opposable-thumb squeeze is modelled.
* **Grasp pipeline**: the sequence consists of lifting the hand clear of the
  table edge, approaching beside the object, descending, sliding the palm in,
  closing the digits, and lifting.
* **The grip is assisted, and this is a shortcut:** when the palm contacts the
  object, a soft weld constraint between hand and object is activated.  In its
  absence, the rigid fingers squeeze the object out (an apple is ejected at
  ~0.4 m/s).  Release deactivates the weld.  Pick-up therefore demonstrates the
  *behaviour* of grasping, and not a physically earned force-closure grasp.
* **Gestures**: `wave, nod, shake_head, shrug, clap, thumbs_up, think, scratch_head, drink, bow`.  **Head and eyes**: neck yaw and pitch together with the eyes
  follow `look_at`, and wander when idle.
* Measured: the sequence grab → hold → put down succeeded for the apple and the
  mug in the scripted test at the table, with the person standing at the table.
  The stone (heavy, wide) could not be gripped, and one mug put-down run ended
  with the mug flung to the floor; the procedure should therefore be regarded as
  fragile.

### 16.4 Speech and thoughts (`speech.py`, `viewer.py`)

Speech is produced by opening and closing the jaw according to a syllable-timed
envelope (no fine mouth shape is produced).  Spoken content is displayed in a
white bubble above the head, and the thoughts of the person are displayed above
the head in grey italics.  The speech can optionally also be rendered aloud
through the Windows Speech Application Programming Interface (SAPI)
(`--voice`).  There is **no built-in drive to speak**; the person speaks only if
the mind decides to do so.

The jaw is moved kinematically, and its actuator is muted while the person
speaks (in an earlier version, the actuator opposed the kinematic override, and
the resulting reaction torque knocked the whole body over).

### 16.5 The mind (`mind.py`, `world.py`, `run_mind.py`, `setup_azr.py`)

The body exposes an application programming interface (API) of actions (`say, look_at, look_forward, hand_pose, wait, walk_to, walk, turn, face, stop, reach, grab, release, put_down, point_at, gesture, crouch, stand`).  `world.py` converts the simulator state into
what a person could know: objects in the field of view, described
egocentrically ("0.4 m ahead, 0.2 m to the right"), together with what the hands
hold and what was heard.  `mind.py` constructs a prompt from this description,
queries a language model, and parses its answer.

* **AZR format.**  The Absolute Zero Reasoner (AZR) is a Qwen2.5-Coder model
  trained with an R1-style `<think>…</think><answer>…</answer>` format.  The
  prompt terminates with `Assistant: <think>`, so that the model continues in
  this format; the text within `<think>` is rendered as the grey italic thought
  bubble, and the `<answer>` is parsed as lines of calls such as
  `look_at("apple")`.  The answer is **never executed**: it is parsed with `ast`
  against a whitelist of the API names, with literal arguments only.
* **Backends**: any OpenAI-compatible `/v1/completions` server (llama.cpp's
  `llama-server`, vLLM, Ollama) with streaming, `llama-cpp-python`, or
  `transformers`.  `setup_azr.py` explains how the weights can be obtained; it
  downloads nothing unless the option `--download` is passed.
* **Incentive.**  The prompt states that the mind does not have to say or do
  anything.  There is no reward for speaking.

**What has and has not been executed.  The AZR model itself has never been run
here.**  No weights are installed, so the live path was verified only against a
small fake OpenAI-compatible server that returns a canned `<think>/<answer>`
stream (`diag_mind_server.py`: prompt construction, Hypertext Transfer Protocol
(HTTP) streaming, parsing, dispatch into the body, the thought bubble, and a
finished `grab('apple')` all function correctly).  In the absence of any model,
`run_mind.py` falls back to a **scripted stand-in** (`StubBackend`), which reacts
to a few keywords and wanders; it is *not* a language model, and the heads-up
display (HUD) states this.  How a real AZR model behaves as this person's mind,
and whether a 3B code-reasoning model is adequate for that role, is unknown.  AZR
was trained for code and mathematical reasoning, and not for embodied dialogue.

```bash
python run_mind.py                                   # window + scripted stand-in
python run_mind.py --at-table --backend server --url http://127.0.0.1:8080/v1
python run_mind.py --headless 45 --at-table --say "4:hello" --say "14:please pick up the apple"
python setup_azr.py                                  # what to do to get AZR
```

### 16.6 Limitations of this part

* Walking is unreliable (§16.2), and stopping and turning are more unreliable
  still.
* Grasping relies on an assist weld; the fingers are rigid, there is no
  opposable thumb, and heavy or wide objects fail.
* The simulation runs at approximately 0.1–0.3× real time on the test machine
  (whole-body control every 4 ms plus rendering), so the window is slow.
* Several fixes were tuned against a single scene layout (table at 0.2 m from the
  edge, objects placed where `build_model.py` positions them).
* The mind receives a symbolic description and not pixels; the 48×36 retina is
  unused by it.
* Diagnostic scripts for this part are `diag_wbc.py`, `diag_walk*.py`,
  `diag_skills.py`, `diag_bubbles.py`, `diag_mind_smoke.py`,
  `diag_mind_server.py`, and `diag_app.py`.
