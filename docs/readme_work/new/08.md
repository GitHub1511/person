## 8. Active inference

```
G(π) = risk  +  ambiguity  −  epistemic value  +  motor cost  +  emotional bias
```

* **Risk** is computed as the divergence between the predicted sensory
  consequence of a policy and the *preferred* observation, which is constructed
  from drives, interoceptive setpoints and the current affective state. An
  agent in a state of fear is accordingly modelled as preferring different
  sensations from those preferred by a curious agent.
* The prediction is treated as a **belief**: the proprioceptive block is
  expected to move most of the way towards the equilibrium point of the policy,
  and this expectation is blended with the learned forward model once the model
  has accumulated data. Because the body must act *before* any learning has
  occurred, policy selection is required to be meaningful from the first tick.
* **Emotional action tendencies** bias whole policy *categories* and are
  deliberately excluded from the free-energy term: an action tendency is a
  low-level disposition and not a deliberative prediction, and keeping the two
  separate is what allows fear to produce a flinch within a fraction of a
  second.
* Policy precision is computed as a softmax whose temperature increases with
  arousal.

The repertoire comprises **55 motor programs** across 11 categories: posture,
balance shifting, reaching, self-touch (hand-to-face, hand-to-chest,
rub-forearm), hand/grasp, gaze, head, face, defensive (flinch, freeze, brace,
step-back), locomotion, exploration.

### Intrinsic motivation

The reward signal comprises ten terms, all of which are internal: **jerk,
energy, stability, novelty, tactile, comfort, affective balance, empowerment,
pain, contact smoothness**. The novelty term rewards *reducible* prediction
error and not noise.
